Profile

  • Computational social scientist studying political communication, disinformation, and hate speech on social media, with a focus on Nepal and Europe. My work combines text-as-data methods, large language models, and multimodal analysis with questions about how citizens encounter and respond to political content. Before moving into social science, I worked for six years as a software engineer, which allows me to build and maintain research pipelines independently.

Projects

  • TikTok Virality and Political Engagement in Nepal's 2022 Local Elections

    MSc thesis, Hertie School (revised 2026). Code · Poster

    • Built a multimodal pipeline (Whisper, GPT-4o image captions and translations, multilingual embeddings) for 2,964 election videos sampled from 28,165.
    • Political attention concentrated among accounts with large audiences, and content about independent candidates drew more attention than general election content. Communication styles were hand-coded with a codebook developed for the study.
  • Reading the Crowd: Perceived Social Consensus and Credibility of Polarized News

    Research proposal for a survey experiment on how source identity and consensus cues shape the credibility of polarized news among young Nepali Facebook users.

  • Online Toxicity and Community Structure on Bluesky

    SICSS-Saarbrücken group project; I led stance classification and network analysis of 156,000 posts.

  • Bias in Toxic Language Detection

    Group project, Deep Learning course, Hertie School; I led fine-tuning and bias evaluation across gender, religion, and race groups.
